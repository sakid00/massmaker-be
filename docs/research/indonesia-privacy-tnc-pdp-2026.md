# Indonesian Legal Requirements: Privacy Policy, Agreement, T&C for Massmaker

**Research date:** 28 September 2026  
**Scope:** Marketplace/platform that collects personal data from users (“makers”/consumers) and vendors (businesses), uses data for marketing (including sharing or using data for vendor marketing), operates in Indonesia (Bahasa Indonesia + English), and will expand categories of personal data later.  
**Method:** Primary sources only. Claims are tied to the statute or regulation that owns them. Secondary press is used only to flag an unverified implementing regulation, not to state article-level rules.  
**This is not legal advice.** Indonesian administrative enforcement of UU 27/2022 depends on a data-protection institution that the statute says the President must establish by Peraturan Presiden; as of this date that institution was not confirmed from official JDIH texts reviewed below.

## Executive summary

Indonesia does **not** name a document called “Privacy Policy” or “Terms & Conditions” as a statutory artefact. What the law actually requires is:

1. **A lawful basis before any processing** of personal data (UU 27 Pasal 20), including collection, analysis, storage, disclosure, transfer, and marketing use (Pasal 16 ayat (1) — *pemrosesan* includes those acts).
2. **Specific, limited, transparent purposes determined at collection** (Pasal 16 ayat (2), Pasal 27–28, and the official *Penjelasan* of “terbatas dan spesifik”).
3. **Pre-collection / pre-consent information** that functions as a privacy notice when processing is based on consent (Pasal 5 + Pasal 21), plus a recorded, explicit consent (Pasal 22, 24). Extra purposes (marketing, sharing with vendors) must be **distinguishable** from the core agreement or the consent is **batal demi hukum** (Pasal 22 ayat (4)–(5), Pasal 23).
4. **Notice to the data subject before changing that information** (Pasal 21 ayat (2)). A clause that says “we may collect additional categories later / change purposes” does **not** replace that duty and is inconsistent with purpose limitation.
5. **Marketplace T&C** must satisfy electronic-contract rules (ITE Pasal 18–20; PP 80 Pasal 50–57), consumer standard-clause bans (UU 8/1999 Pasal 18), identity and honest information (PP 80 Pasal 13; PP 71 Pasal 29), and **Bahasa Indonesia** for contracts aimed at Indonesian consumers (PP 80 Pasal 55; UU 24/2009 Pasal 31; Perpres 63/2019 Pasal 26).
6. **Sharing user data with vendors for the vendors’ marketing** is *transfer / pengungkapan* (Pasal 16 ayat (1) huruf e). It needs its own basis, usually **separate explicit consent**, plus controller-to-controller or processor contracts (Pasal 18, 51, 55). Bundling it into a single “I agree to the T&C” click is the main legal risk.
7. **Sanctions that policies should not understate:** administrative (warning, suspend processing, delete data, fine up to **2% of annual revenue** — Pasal 57); criminal for unlawful obtain/disclose/use (Pasal 65–70); consumer-law criminal for prohibited *klausula baku* and related offences (UU 8/1999 Pasal 62). The UU 27 administrative enforcer (*lembaga*, Pasal 58) is to be established by Perpres; that gap does **not** suspend the obligations in Pasal 74 after 17 October 2024.

## Applicable laws and hierarchy

Indonesian hierarchy (UU No. 12 Tahun 2011 as amended, not re-quoted here): UUD 1945 → UU/Perppu → PP → Perpres → Permen.

For Massmaker as an Indonesian marketplace / *Penyelenggara Sistem Elektronik Lingkup Privat* / *PPMSE*:

1. **UU 27/2022** is the general personal-data statute. It applies to anyone in Indonesia, and to processing outside Indonesia that has legal effect in Indonesia or on Indonesian data subjects (Pasal 2). It applies to **orang perseorangan** data, not to purely corporate non-personal data. Vendor *contact persons* and sole traders are still *Subjek Data Pribadi*.
2. **UU 27 Pasal 75:** older PDP rules (notably Permenkominfo 20/2016 and the PDP sections of PP 71/2019) **remain in force insofar as they do not contradict** UU 27. Where they conflict (e.g. Permen 20 treating consent as the default basis), **UU 27’s six bases in Pasal 20 control**.
3. **PP 71/2019** still governs electronic-system operators (registration via Permen 5/2020, user-facing information in Pasal 29, data-processing principles in Pasal 14).
4. **PP 80/2019 + Permendag 31/2023** still govern *Perdagangan Melalui Sistem Elektronik*. BPK still lists PP 80 as **Berlaku**. No replacement PP was found.
5. **ITE (UU 11/2008 as amended through UU 1/2024)** governs electronic information, electronic transactions, clickwrap formation, child protection for PSE (Pasal 16A), and Indonesian law for certain international standard-form e-contracts (Pasal 18A).
6. **UU 8/1999** governs consumer-facing T&C (*klausula baku*), advertising, and information duties. Violation of Pasal 18 is a **criminal** offence (Pasal 62 ayat (1)).
7. **UU 24/2009 + Perpres 63/2019** require Bahasa Indonesia in agreements involving an Indonesian private entity or Indonesian individual.

## Privacy Policy mandatory content

Indonesian law never says “publish a privacy policy.” It requires **information to the data subject** and, where consent is the basis, a **listed set of disclosures before consent**. A Privacy Policy (Kebijakan Privasi) is the practical vehicle. If processing is **not** based on consent (e.g. Pasal 20 ayat (2) huruf b — performing a contract), Pasal 21’s list does not itself trigger, but Pasal 5, Pasal 16 ayat (2), and Pasal 27–28 still require identity, purpose, and transparency.

### A. Right to information (always)

**UU 27 Pasal 5:** *Subjek Data Pribadi berhak mendapatkan Informasi tentang kejelasan identitas, dasar kepentingan hukum, tujuan permintaan dan penggunaan Data Pribadi, dan akuntabilitas pihak yang meminta Data Pribadi.*

So the notice must make clear:

- **Identity** of the party requesting/processing (controller).
- **Legal basis** (*dasar kepentingan hukum*).
- **Purpose** of the request and of the use.
- **Accountability** of that party (who is responsible; how to reach them).

Official *Penjelasan* Pasal 16 ayat (2) huruf a: *transparan* means the data subject **already knows** what data is processed and how, and that related information is **easy to access and understand, in clear language**.

### B. Mandatory disclosures when the basis is consent

**UU 27 Pasal 21 ayat (1):** if processing is based on Pasal 20 ayat (2) huruf a (*persetujuan*), the controller **wajib** convey information about:

| Huruf | Required content |
|---|---|
| a | *legalitas dari pemrosesan Data Pribadi* |
| b | *tujuan pemrosesan Data Pribadi* |
| c | *jenis dan relevansi Data Pribadi yang akan diproses* |
| d | *jangka waktu retensi dokumen yang memuat Data Pribadi* |
| e | *rincian mengenai Informasi yang dikumpulkan* |
| f | *jangka waktu pemrosesan Data Pribadi* |
| g | *hak Subjek Data Pribadi* |

Official *Penjelasan* Pasal 21: *“rincian mengenai Informasi yang dikumpulkan”* is a **list** of personal data, both general and specific, collected for the processing. *“jangka waktu pemrosesan”* is the span from start to finish of the processing activities according to the purpose.

**Pasal 21 ayat (2):** if that information **changes**, the controller **wajib memberitahukan kepada Subjek Data Pribadi sebelum terjadi perubahan Informasi.** Notice must precede the change. This is the statutory rule on policy updates (see section below).

### C. What “personal data” covers (so the policy can list categories honestly)

**Pasal 1 angka 1:** *Data Pribadi* is data about an identified or identifiable natural person, alone or combined with other information, electronic or not.

**Pasal 4** splits:

- **Specific:** health; biometric; genetic; criminal record; **children’s data**; personal financial data; and/or other data as provided by law.
- **General:** full name; sex; nationality; religion; marital status; and/or data combined to identify a person.

Official *Penjelasan* Pasal 4 ayat (3) huruf f: combined identifiers include **mobile number and IP address**.

A marketplace that stores email, phone, address, device/IP, order history, or vendor KYC of a natural person is processing *Data Pribadi*. Children’s data is *spesifik* even if the fields look ordinary.

### D. Controller identification and contact

From Pasal 5 (identity + accountability) plus:

- **PP 71 Pasal 29:** a PSE **wajib** give users at least: (a) identity of the PSE; (b) object of the transaction; (c) fitness/security of the system; (d) how to use the device; (e) **syarat kontrak**; (f) procedure to reach agreement; (g) **jaminan privasi dan/atau pelindungan Data Pribadi**; (h) **complaint telephone number**.
- **Permenkominfo 20/2016 Pasal 28 huruf i:** the PSE must provide a *narahubung (contact person)* that the data owner can easily contact about management of their data.
- **Permendag 31/2023 Pasal 9–10:** PPMSE must display consumer-complaint contact clearly, plus Ditjen PKTN complaint contact.

The Privacy Policy should therefore name: legal entity, address, email/phone of the data-protection contact (and DPO if Pasal 53 applies), and a complaint path.

### E. Data subject rights the policy must describe (Pasal 21 ayat (1) huruf g)

UU 27 Bab IV (Pasal 5–15) and controller duties that implement them:

| Right | Pasal | Controller operational duty |
|---|---|---|
| Information | 5 | See above. |
| Complete / update / correct | 6 | Pasal 30: update/correct within **3×24 hours** of request; notify the result. |
| Access and copy | 7 | Pasal 32: access to the data **and processing log**, within **3×24 hours**. Penjelasan: copy is free except certain cost cases. |
| End processing / erase / destroy | 8 | Pasal 42–45. |
| Withdraw consent | 9 | Pasal 40: stop processing within **3×24 hours** of withdrawal. |
| Object to solely automated decisions including profiling | 10 | Further procedure “diatur dalam PP” — **PP not verified**. |
| Restrict / delay processing | 11 | Pasal 41: within **3×24 hours**, with listed exceptions (including a written contract that makes restriction impossible). |
| Sue and receive damages | 12 | Procedure “diatur dalam PP” — **PP not verified**. |
| Portability | 13 | Further rules “diatur dalam PP” — **PP not verified**. |

Requests under Pasal 6–11 are filed by **recorded application**, electronic or not (Pasal 14).

### F. Other content the statute requires you to be able to explain (even if not labelled “privacy policy fields”)

- **Lawful basis per purpose** (Pasal 20) — not one blanket basis for everything.
- **Recipients / transfer:** processing includes *transfer, penyebarluasan, pengungkapan* (Pasal 16 ayat (1) huruf e). Domestic transfer: Pasal 55 (both sender and recipient must protect). Cross-border: Pasal 56 (adequacy → binding adequate protection → **consent**).
- **Processors:** Pasal 51 — processor acts on controller instructions; written approval before sub-processors; controller remains responsible unless the processor exceeds instructions.
- **Joint controllers:** Pasal 18 — written agreement on roles, related purposes, and a joint contact.
- **Security:** Pasal 35, 36, 38, 39 (technical-operational measures; confidentiality; prevent unlawful processing and unauthorised access). Permen 20/2016 Pasal 15 ayat (2) still requires **encrypted** stored personal data and a **minimum 5-year** retention if no sectoral period applies — that retention floor can conflict with UU 27’s “delete when purpose ends” (Pasal 42–43) unless the 5 years is itself a legal retention. PP 80 Pasal 38 requires PMSE records: **10 years** for financial-transaction data, **5 years** for other PMSE data. Draft retention to the longest applicable legal hold, then delete.
- **Records of processing:** Pasal 31 (*wajib melakukan perekaman terhadap seluruh kegiatan pemrosesan*).
- **Breach notice:** Pasal 46 — written notice within **3×24 hours** to the data subject **and** *lembaga*, stating what data leaked, when/how, and remediation. In certain cases, public notice. (Who receives the *lembaga* copy is unclear until the institution exists.)
- **DPIA:** Pasal 34 — mandatory if processing has **high risk**, including: automated decisions with legal/significant effect; **specific data**; **large-scale** processing; systematic evaluation/scoring/monitoring; matching/combining datasets; **new technology**; or processing that limits data-subject rights. A marketplace that profiles users for vendor marketing likely hits several of these. Detail “diatur dalam PP” — **PP not verified**; the trigger list in ayat (2) is already in the UU.
- **DPO (*pejabat atau petugas Pelindungan Data Pribadi*):** Pasal 53 — mandatory if (a) processing for public service; **or** (b) core activities require **regular and systematic monitoring of personal data on a large scale**; **or** (c) core activities are large-scale processing of **specific** data and/or criminal-related data. A marketplace whose core business includes monitoring user behaviour for matching/marketing is at least in the Pasal 53 ayat (1) huruf b conversation. Detail “diatur dalam PP” — **PP not verified**.
- **Children:** Pasal 25 — children’s data is processed **specially** and **requires consent of the parent and/or guardian**. ITE Pasal 16A (UU 1/2024) additionally requires PSE to protect children, with **minimum-age information, age verification, and a child-rights abuse reporting mechanism**. Implementing PP for 16A ayat (5) **not verified**.
- **Disability:** Pasal 26 — special communication methods; consent of the person and/or guardian.
- **Cookies / tracking:** **No Indonesian statute uses the word “cookie.”** If a cookie, pixel, SDK, or `localStorage` value is or can be combined into *Data Pribadi* (Pasal 1 angka 1; *Penjelasan* Pasal 4 mentions IP address), ordinary UU 27 rules apply: basis, purpose, Pasal 5/16/21 information. There is **no** ePrivacy-style “cookie banner” geometry rule. See also companion note in the workspace cookie research; this file does not rely on it as a source.

### G. Permenkominfo 20/2016 extras still useful where not contrary to UU 27

- **Pasal 7:** consent form **in Bahasa Indonesia**.
- **Pasal 21:** display/announce/send/disseminate/open access only **with consent**, unless another law says otherwise, **including between PSEs, PSE and user, or users**.
- **Pasal 28 huruf f:** **option** for the owner whether data may be used and/or displayed by/on a **third party**, still tied to the original collection purpose.
- **Pasal 2 ayat (2) huruf g / Pasal 5:** internal personal-data rules.

These are the closest positive-law requirements to a “privacy notice + third-party sharing toggle.”

## Consent, purpose limitation, and marketing/vendor data sharing

### Lawful bases (UU 27 Pasal 20)

The controller **wajib memiliki dasar pemrosesan** before processing. Bases (ayat (2)):

| Huruf | Basis | Typical marketplace use |
|---|---|---|
| a | **Explicit valid consent** for one or several **specified** purposes already communicated | Marketing; sharing with vendors; non-essential tracking |
| b | Performance of a **contract** to which the data subject is party, or steps at the data subject’s request before contracting | Account, order, delivery, billing needed to run the requested service |
| c | Legal obligation of the controller | Tax invoices, PMSE record-keeping (PP 80 Pasal 38) |
| d | Vital interests | Emergency only |
| e | Public task / public service / statutory authority | Generally not a private marketplace |
| f | **Other legitimate interests**, balancing the controller’s purpose/needs against the data subject’s rights | **Not defined further in the UU.** No verified PP explains when B2C marketing or selling leads to vendors qualifies. Do not treat huruf f as a silent licence to share data with vendors. |

**PP 71 Pasal 14 ayat (3)** (still in force via Pasal 75 UU 27 unless contrary): processing must have *persetujuan yang sah* for one or more specified purposes **already communicated**. Ayat (4) then lists the same family of alternative bases as UU 27. Official *Penjelasan* Pasal 14 ayat (3): *“persetujuan yang sah” adalah persetujuan yang disampaikan secara eksplisit, tidak boleh secara tersembunyi atau atas dasar kekhilafan, kelalaian, atau paksaan.*

So: **hidden, implied, or bundled-in-the-fine-print consent is not “sah.”**

### Form of consent (UU 27 Pasal 22, 24, 9, 40)

- Written **or recorded**; electronic or not; equal legal force (Pasal 22 ayat (1)–(3)).
- If the consent **contains another purpose**, the request must be (a) **clearly distinguishable** from other matters; (b) in an understandable, easily accessible format; (c) in **simple, clear language** (Pasal 22 ayat (4)). Official *Penjelasan*: *“bahasa” adalah Bahasa Indonesia.*
- Consent that fails ayat (1) or (4) is **batal demi hukum** (ayat (5)).
- **Pasal 23:** a **contract clause** that requests processing **without explicit valid consent** is **batal demi hukum**.
- Controller must **be able to show** the consent (Pasal 24).
- Withdrawal is a right (Pasal 9); processing based on that consent must stop within **3×24 hours** (Pasal 40).

**Practical consequence:** “I agree to the Terms and the Privacy Policy” as a single checkbox **cannot** validly authorise vendor-marketing sharing. That extra purpose must be a separate, Bahasa Indonesia, recorded opt-in. Pre-ticked boxes are inconsistent with “eksplisit” (PP 71 *Penjelasan* Pasal 14 ayat (3) + Pasal 22).

### Purpose limitation

- Pasal 16 ayat (2) huruf a–b: collection **limited and specific, lawful, transparent**; processing **according to its purpose**.
- Pasal 27: processing **terbatas dan spesifik, sah, transparan**.
- Pasal 28: processing **sesuai dengan tujuan**.
- Official *Penjelasan* of “secara terbatas dan spesifik”: collection must be limited to the processing purpose, and **the purpose must be explicit, lawful, and already determined at the time of collection**.

**PP 80 Pasal 59 ayat (2)** (PMSE-specific, still in force) is even more granular:

- data obtained fairly and lawfully from the owner, **with a choice** and security guarantees;
- held only for **one or more purposes described specifically and lawfully**, and **not processed further in a manner incompatible with that purpose**;
- adequate, relevant, **not excessive**;
- accurate and kept up to date;
- not kept longer than needed;
- processed according to the owner’s rights;
- security against leakage and unlawful use;
- **not sent outside Indonesia unless the Minister has declared the destination has equivalent protection** (huruf h).

Huruf h of PP 80 Pasal 59 is **stricter and older** than UU 27 Pasal 56 (adequacy **or** binding adequate protection **or** consent). UU 27 is the later special PDP statute (Pasal 75: older rules remain if not contrary). There is a **real tension** on cross-border transfers. Conservative drafting: satisfy **Pasal 56 UU 27** and do not transfer on a hope that PP 80’s ministerial whitelist exists — this research did **not** retrieve a published Menteri Perdagangan adequacy list. If a destination is not adequate, use binding protection or **consent** (Pasal 56 ayat (3)–(4)).

### Sharing or using consumer data for vendor marketing

Acts that are “pemrosesan” (Pasal 16 ayat (1) huruf e): *penampilan, pengumuman, **transfer**, **penyebarluasan**, atau **pengungkapan***. Giving a vendor a list of makers’ emails, phone numbers, purchase interests, or lookalike audiences is transfer/disclosure.

What the texts actually require:

1. **A basis** (Pasal 20). Contract (huruf b) covers only what is needed to perform **that** user’s contract (e.g. pass shipping data to the fulfilling vendor for **that order**). It does **not** extend to “so vendors can market later.”
2. **If consent:** Pasal 21 list + Pasal 22 separate request in Bahasa Indonesia + Pasal 24 proof + Pasal 9/40 withdrawal. Name the **categories of recipients** (vendors / advertisers) — otherwise “jenis dan relevansi” and transparency fail.
3. **If the vendor determines its own marketing purposes** after receiving the data, Massmaker and the vendor may be **joint controllers** (Pasal 18: written agreement on roles, related purposes, joint contact) or successive controllers (Pasal 55: both must protect). If the vendor only processes on Massmaker’s documented instructions, the vendor is a **processor** (Pasal 51) and needs a processing contract; using the data for the vendor’s own ads would exceed instructions and shift liability to the vendor (Pasal 51 ayat (6)) **and** still be unlawful vis-à-vis the consumer if no basis exists.
4. **Permen 20/2016 Pasal 21 and Pasal 28 huruf f** (still relevant if not contrary): third-party access/display requires consent and an **option** whether data may be used/displayed by a third party, still tied to the original purpose.
5. **Permendag 31/2023 Pasal 13 ayat (3)** (from the official Kemendag PDF): PPMSE must ensure there is **no abuse of control of user data** for use by the PPMSE and/or affiliated companies in a manner that harms fair competition. That is a trade/competition overlay on top of PDP: even “consented” sharing that distorts competition can be a Permendag violation (administrative sanctions in Pasal 50, 58).

**Direct marketing to the user by Massmaker itself** still needs a basis. Pasal 20 huruf f is theoretically available; the UU does not say “marketing = legitimate interest.” Given Pasal 22’s anti-bundling rule and PP 71’s “not hidden” consent, the legally honest path for **optional** marketing is **separate opt-in consent**, with withdrawal (Pasal 9).

There is **no** statutory “notice + opt-out is enough for B2C marketing” rule in UU 27. Inventing an opt-out-only model would be importing foreign law.

### Profiling for marketing

Pasal 10: right to object to decisions **based solely on automated processing, including profiling**, that have legal effects or **significant impact**. Official *Penjelasan* Pasal 10: *pemrofilan* includes identifying a person by, among other things, job history, economic condition, health, personal preferences, interests, reliability, behaviour, location, or movement, electronically.

Pasal 34 ayat (2) huruf a and d: DPIA if automated decisions with significant effect, or systematic evaluation/scoring/monitoring.

Implementing procedure for the objection (Pasal 10 ayat (2)) is **in a PP that was not verified**. The right itself is already in force.

## Updating policies and adding new data categories

### What the statute actually says

**Pasal 21 ayat (2):** *Dalam hal terdapat perubahan Informasi sebagaimana dimaksud pada ayat (1), Pengendali Data Pribadi wajib memberitahukan kepada Subjek Data Pribadi sebelum terjadi perubahan Informasi.*

The “Informasi” is the Pasal 21 ayat (1) list: legality, **purpose**, **types and relevance of data**, retention, **details of information collected**, processing period, and rights.

So: adding a new category of data, lengthening retention, adding a new purpose (vendor marketing), or adding a new class of recipients is a **change of Informasi**. Notice must go out **before** the change takes effect.

That duty is written for **consent-based** processing (chapeau of Pasal 21). For other bases, Pasal 5 + Pasal 16 ayat (2) huruf f still require notifying **purpose and processing activity**. Official *Penjelasan* of transparency: the subject must **know** what is processed and how.

**Purpose limitation** (Pasal 16(2)(b), 27, 28, *Penjelasan* “tujuan … telah ditentukan pada saat pengumpulan”): you cannot collect “for purposes we will decide later.”

**Pasal 22 ayat (4)–(5) and Pasal 23:** a new purpose needs a **new, distinguishable, recorded consent** if you rely on consent; stuffing it into an old T&C is void.

**Pasal 43 ayat (1) huruf a:** delete when data is **no longer needed for the purpose**. If you invent a new purpose, old data is not automatically reusable.

### Are “we may collect additional data in the future” clauses valid?

**Not as a substitute for Pasal 21 ayat (2) + purpose limitation.** The UU requires purposes and data types to be determined **at collection**, and **prior notice** of changes to that information. A prospective waiver:

- does not list *jenis dan relevansi* or *rincian Informasi* (Pasal 21 ayat (1) huruf c, e);
- is not a purpose “determined at collection” (*Penjelasan* Pasal 27);
- if used to skip a new consent for a new optional purpose, is *batal demi hukum* (Pasal 22 ayat (5), Pasal 23);
- if placed in consumer T&C as a unilateral right to change rules while the consumer is using the service, is also a prohibited *klausula baku* under **UU 8/1999 Pasal 18 ayat (1) huruf g** (consumer may not be made to submit to new/additional/amended rules made unilaterally during the period they use the service). Such a clause is **batal demi hukum** (Pasal 18 ayat (3)).

### Lawful approach (what the texts support)

| Change | What UU 27 / UU 8 actually support |
|---|---|
| New **optional** purpose (vendor marketing, extra analytics) | **New distinguishable consent** (Pasal 22) after Pasal 21 information; keep the old processing under the old purpose; do not condition the core service on the extra consent in a way that makes consent involuntary (Pasal 22 ayat (4) + PP 71 *Penjelasan* “tidak … paksaan”). |
| New **category of data** needed to perform the same contract | **Prior notice** (Pasal 21 ayat (2) and/or Pasal 5/16). If the basis is huruf b, collect only what the new contractual feature actually needs. If users can refuse the new feature, do not collect. |
| New category that is **specific data** (Pasal 4 ayat (2)), e.g. biometrics, children’s data, financial | Higher bar: DPIA (Pasal 34), possibly DPO (Pasal 53), parental consent if children (Pasal 25). Do not hide in a T&C update. |
| Recipients / overseas processors | Prior notice; Pasal 55/56; processor contract (Pasal 51). Cross-border: adequacy, binding protection, or consent (Pasal 56). |
| Retention change | Prior notice; cannot keep longer than purpose + legal holds (Pasal 42–44; PP 80 Pasal 38). |
| “Continued use = consent to the new policy” | **Not** a form recognised as *persetujuan tertulis atau terekam* that is distinguishable (Pasal 22). Conflicts with Pasal 18 ayat (1) huruf g UU 8/1999 if it binds consumers to unilateral new rules. |

There is **no** verified Indonesian statutory “30-day notice then implied consent” rule. If you give prior notice (Pasal 21 ayat (2)) and the user **does nothing**, you still lack new consent for a new consent-based purpose. For a contract-necessary change, you may need to offer a choice to stop using the service rather than invent implied consent.

**Pasal 48:** if the controller (a legal entity) merges, splits, is acquired, consolidates, or dissolves, it **wajib** notify data subjects of the transfer of personal data **before and after** the corporate event. Further procedure “diatur dalam PP” — **PP not verified**; the duty to notify is already in the UU.

## Terms & Conditions / User Agreement requirements

Indonesian law treats T&C as **klausula baku** and/or **Kontrak Elektronik**, not as a privacy instrument. Privacy consents that are only inside T&C are legally fragile (Pasal 22–23 UU 27).

### A. Formation (clickwrap is recognised)

**ITE Pasal 18 ayat (1):** an electronic transaction embodied in an electronic contract **binds the parties**.

**ITE Pasal 19:** parties must use an electronic system **they have agreed**. *Penjelasan:* “disepakati” includes agreeing the **procedures in the system**.

**ITE Pasal 20:** unless the parties provide otherwise, the transaction occurs when the offer sent by the sender **has been received and approved** by the recipient; approval **must** be an **electronic statement of acceptance**.

**PP 80 Pasal 1** (definitions used below): *Kontrak Elektronik* is an agreement made through an electronic system; *Konfirmasi Elektronik* is giving the buyer/user a conscious chance to accept or reject according to the technical mechanism and the terms in the electronic offer, **before** the contract is deemed formed.

**PP 80 Pasal 50–52:** PMSE may use an electronic contract or another contractual mechanism. An electronic contract is valid and binding if: it matches the terms of the electronic offer; information in the contract matches the offer; there is agreement (offer terms sent, received, **and approved**); parties have capacity; there is a certain subject-matter; the object is not contrary to law, morality, or public order. That last cluster tracks **KUHPerdata Pasal 1320** (consent, capacity, certain object, lawful cause).

**PP 80 Pasal 44–47** (electronic offer / acceptance / confirmation): the offer must contain minimum information; if the offeree is not responsive and does not follow the acceptance procedure, the contract **may be deemed never formed**; confirmation may include identifying/correcting form data or a statement that the user has enough information and clearly intends to buy. **Silent browse-wrap without an electronic acceptance is a poor fit** for Pasal 20 ITE and PP 80’s *Konfirmasi Elektronik*.

**ITE Pasal 18A (inserted by UU 1/2024):** an **international** electronic contract that uses **standard clauses** made by a PSE is **governed by Indonesian law** if: (a) the user is from Indonesia and gives consent from/in Indonesian jurisdiction; **and/or** (b) performance is in Indonesia; **and/or** (c) the PSE has a place of business or conducts business in Indonesia. Ayat (2): such a contract must use language that is **simple, clear, and easy to understand**, and uphold good faith and transparency. This is aimed at foreign platforms’ clickwrap; a domestic Massmaker T&C should still meet the same clarity standard.

**ITE Pasal 17 ayat (2) (as amended):** parties to an electronic transaction **wajib beriktikad baik** during the interaction/exchange of electronic information.

### B. Mandatory content of a consumer electronic contract (PP 80 Pasal 53)

The contract must match the offer and contain **at least**:

1. identity of the parties;
2. specification of the goods and/or services agreed;
3. legality of the goods and/or services;
4. transaction value;
5. payment terms and period;
6. operational procedure for delivery;
7. return procedure if the goods/services do not match what was agreed;
8. cancellation procedure;
9. **choice of law for PMSE dispute resolution**.

**Pasal 53 ayat (2):** the contract **may not** contain *klausula baku* that harm consumers as regulated in the Consumer Protection Law.

**Pasal 54:** electronic signature may be used as the mark of agreement, per ITE rules.

**Pasal 55:** electronic contracts **aimed at consumers in Indonesia must use Bahasa Indonesia.**

**Pasal 56:** the trader **wajib** provide a contract the consumer can **download and/or save**.

**Pasal 57:** the contract is automatically **batal demi hukum** if a technical error occurs because the system is not secure, reliable, and responsible.

**PP 80 Pasal 13:** in every PMSE, the trader must give **true, clear, honest** information about the identity of the legal subject (supported by valid data/documents) and about the condition and warranties of the goods/services **including the electronic system used**.

**PP 71 Pasal 29** (PSE, overlapping): identity, object, security, how to use, **syarat kontrak**, how agreement is reached, privacy guarantee, complaint phone.

### C. Prohibited standard clauses (UU 8/1999 Pasal 18) — T&C drafting red lines

**Pasal 18 ayat (1):** a business actor offering goods/services for trade **may not** make or include *klausula baku* that, among other things:

- shift the business actor’s responsibility (huruf a);
- allow refusal to take back goods (b) or refunds (c);
- grant the business actor a unilateral power of attorney over instalment goods (d);
- reverse the burden of proof about loss of usefulness (e);
- let the business actor reduce the benefit of a service or the consumer’s assets that are the object of the sale (f);
- **state that the consumer submits to new, additional, further, and/or amended rules made unilaterally by the business actor while the consumer is using the service** (g);
- grant power to encumber instalment goods (h).

**Ayat (2):** may not use standard clauses whose placement or form is hard to see, unreadable, or whose wording is hard to understand.

**Ayat (3):** any such clause is **batal demi hukum**.

**Ayat (4):** business actors must bring existing clauses into line with this Law.

**Pasal 62 ayat (1):** violating Pasal 18 is punishable by imprisonment of up to **5 years** or a fine of up to **Rp 2.000.000.000**. Policies that “waive all liability,” “we may change these terms at any time and continued use means you agree,” or hide marketing consent in grey 8-pt text, collide with Pasal 18 and with UU 27 Pasal 22–23 at the same time.

**Pasal 4 huruf c / Pasal 7 huruf b:** consumers have a right to **true, clear, honest** information; business actors must provide it.

**Pasal 9–10, 17:** false or misleading promotion/advertising is prohibited (relevant if T&C or ads overclaim how data is used).

### D. User Agreement vs T&C vs Privacy Policy (what *must* live where)

The statutes do not prescribe three document titles. Mapping duties to documents is a **drafting choice**. What must not be collapsed:

| Duty | Natural document | Why not only T&C |
|---|---|---|
| Pasal 21 information + recorded consent for optional processing | **Privacy Policy + separate consent UI** | Pasal 22 ayat (4): extra purposes must be distinguishable; Pasal 23 voids T&C processing requests without explicit consent. |
| Account/order/payment/delivery rules, fees, cancellation, IP, acceptable use | **T&C / User Agreement** (Kontrak Elektronik) | PP 80 Pasal 53 list; ITE Pasal 18–20. |
| Vendor onboarding, commission, KYC, content standards | **Vendor agreement** (may be a separate Kontrak Elektronik) | Different parties and purposes; vendor natural persons still have Pasal 5/21 rights. |
| Joint-controller / processor terms with vendors | **Data-processing / joint-controller addendum** | Pasal 18, 51, 55 — not consumer T&C. |
| Cookie/tracker list if those items are personal data | Privacy Policy (and consent UI if consent is the basis) | No cookie statute; UU 27 still applies. |

Consumer T&C should **link** to the Privacy Policy and **not** purport to grant marketing/share-with-vendor rights.

### E. Marketplace-specific operator duties that T&C cannot contract out of

- **Permenkominfo 5/2020:** PSE Lingkup Privat **wajib** register **before** the system is used (Pasal 2–3 area of the JDIH text); **wajib menyediakan petunjuk penggunaan layanan dalam bahasa Indonesia**.
- **Permendag 31/2023 Pasal 3, 6:** PPMSE generally needs PMSE business licensing via OSS.
- **PP 80 Pasal 38:** keep PMSE records 10 years (financial) / 5 years (other).
- **PP 80 Pasal 58–59:** personal data treated as the owner’s personal right; the trader is a **trustee** (*pengemban amanat*) in storing and controlling it; deletion on request when the owner leaves/unsubscribes (Pasal 59 ayat (3)–(4)).

## Language, form, and accessibility (web/PDF)

### Bahasa Indonesia is mandatory in several overlapping rules

| Rule | What must be in Bahasa Indonesia |
|---|---|
| UU 27 *Penjelasan* of “bahasa” (attached to the consent-language requirement) | Consent / the Pasal 22 ayat (4) request: *“Yang dimaksud dengan ‘bahasa’ adalah Bahasa Indonesia.”* |
| Permenkominfo 20/2016 **Pasal 7** | **Consent form** requesting the owner’s consent. |
| PP 80 **Pasal 55** | **Kontrak Elektronik aimed at consumers in Indonesia.** |
| UU 24/2009 **Pasal 31 ayat (1)** (wording reproduced in Perpres 63/2019 Pasal 26 ayat (1) official PDF) | *Nota kesepahaman atau perjanjian* involving a state body, Indonesian government agency, **Indonesian private entity, or Indonesian individual**. |
| UU 24/2009 Pasal 31 ayat (2) / Perpres 63/2019 Pasal 26 ayat (2)–(4) | If a foreign party is involved, also write the foreign national language and/or English, as a **translation/equivalent** of the Indonesian; if interpretations diverge, use the language **agreed in the instrument**. |
| Permenkominfo 5/2020 | **Petunjuk penggunaan layanan** in Bahasa Indonesia. |
| UU 8/1999 Pasal 8 ayat (1) huruf j | Product-use information in Bahasa Indonesia (goods context; still signals the consumer-language policy). |
| Permendag 31/2023 Pasal 5 ayat (2) | Foreign merchants on a PPMSE: **Bahasa Indonesia that is easy to understand** on goods/services descriptions. |
| ITE Pasal 18A ayat (2) | International standard-form e-contracts: simple, clear, easy to understand (language of drafting, not only translation). |

**English-only T&C or Privacy Policy for Indonesian consumers is non-compliant** with PP 80 Pasal 55 and with Pasal 7 Permen 20/2016 (consent). Dual language is allowed: Indonesian as the legally operative consumer text; English as translation (Perpres 63/2019 Pasal 26). Say which language prevails on conflict, consistent with Pasal 26 ayat (4).

**SEMA 3/2023** (Mahkamah Agung guidance, 29 Nov 2023): an Indonesian private entity or individual who contracts with a **foreign party** in a foreign language **without** an Indonesian translation **cannot use that alone to annul** the contract, unless absence of the translation is due to a party’s **bad faith**. Source: Kepaniteraan MA announcement of the rumusan. This **does not repeal** Pasal 31 UU 24/2009 and **does not** authorise English-only consumer clickwrap by an Indonesian platform. It is a litigation-risk note for B2B contracts with foreigners.

### Form

- Consent: written or **recorded**, electronic allowed (UU 27 Pasal 22). Keep logs (Pasal 24, 31).
- T&C: consumer must be able to **download and/or store** the electronic contract (PP 80 Pasal 56). A web page that cannot be saved is a gap; offering **PDF or durable download** matches Pasal 56.
- Accessibility of wording: UU 8/1999 Pasal 18 ayat (2) (readable); ITE Pasal 18A ayat (2); UU 27 Pasal 22 ayat (4) and *Penjelasan* “transparan.”
- Disability: UU 27 Pasal 26 — special communication methods; do not rely on a single inaccessible PDF.

### Web vs app

No statute requires a particular URL path. PP 71 Pasal 29 and Permendag 31/2023 Pasal 9–10 require identity, contract terms, privacy guarantee, and complaint contacts to be **available to the user**. Putting them only in an unpublished PDF fails transparency (*Penjelasan* Pasal 16: easy to access).

## Gaps / risks for a marketplace that uses user data for vendor marketing

1. **Bundled consent in T&C.** Highest-probability voidness: Pasal 22 ayat (4)–(5), Pasal 23 UU 27, plus Pasal 18 UU 8/1999. Vendor-marketing sharing needs a **separate Bahasa Indonesia opt-in**, named recipients, and withdrawal.
2. **“Future categories / we may update this policy” as a blank cheque.** Conflicts with purpose limitation (*Penjelasan* Pasal 27), Pasal 21 ayat (2), and Pasal 18 ayat (1) huruf g UU 8/1999. Lawful path: **prior notice + new consent for new optional purposes**; do not recycle old data for a new purpose (Pasal 43 ayat (1) huruf a).
3. **Role confusion (controller vs processor vs joint controller).** If vendors market for their own purposes, Pasal 18/55/51 paperwork is missing in many marketplaces. Without it, Massmaker remains the party that “determined the purpose” (Pasal 1 angka 4) and stays liable (Pasal 47, 51 ayat (3)).
4. **Huruf f (legitimate interest) used for lead-sharing.** The UU supplies the basis in the abstract and **does not** operationalise a marketing balancing test. A verified implementing PP was **not** found. Relying on huruf f for third-party marketing is an **untested statutory gap**, not a documented safe harbour.
5. **Cross-border tools** (email ESPs, analytics, ads, cloud). Pasal 56 vs PP 80 Pasal 59 ayat (2) huruf h. Adequacy lists by the PDP *lembaga* (Pasal 60 angka 6) and by the Trade Minister (PP 80) were **not** retrieved. Default to **binding protection and/or consent**, and say so honestly in the notice.
6. **DPO and DPIA.** Pasal 53/34 likely engaged by large-scale monitoring and marketing profiles. Further procedure is in an **unverified PP**. The duties to appoint (if the thresholds are met) and to assess high-risk processing are already in the UU.
7. **Children.** If “makers” can be under 18, Pasal 25 (parent/guardian consent) and ITE Pasal 16A (age floor, verification, reporting) apply. 16A’s implementing PP is **not verified**. Specific data (Pasal 4 ayat (2) huruf e) raises DPIA (Pasal 34 ayat (2) huruf b).
8. **Enforcement gap vs obligation.** Pasal 57 administrative fines are imposed by *lembaga* (Pasal 58–60), which the UU says the President establishes by Perpres. JDIH Kemkomdigi still lists no implementing regulation for UU 27. **Pasal 74’s two-year adjustment has ended.** Criminal provisions (Pasal 65–70) do not depend on that institution. Consumer-law and Kominfo/Kemendag administrative tracks (PP 71, Permen 5/2020, Permen 20/2016, Permendag 31/2023) remain available.
9. **PP 80 Pasal 58** calls personal data the owner’s “hak milik pribadi” and the trader a trustee. That sits beside UU 27’s rights model. It is extra reason not to treat a marketplace user database as a vendor-marketing asset.
10. **Cookies.** No cookie law. Tracking IDs + IP can still be *Data Pribadi* (*Penjelasan* Pasal 4). Do not claim “cookies are unregulated therefore free.”
11. **Unverified “PP 33/2026”.** Do not draft to blog summaries of circulating copies. Re-check JDIH/peraturan.go.id before treating any implementing PP as in force. If an official PP is later gazetted with a delayed effective date, diary that date; **UU 27 already binds**.

## Practical drafting checklist

Use this as a build list. Each item maps to a cited duty.

### Privacy Policy (Kebijakan Privasi) — Bahasa Indonesia first; English translation optional

- [ ] Legal name, address, and contact of the **Pengendali Data Pribadi**; DPO/petugas contact if Pasal 53 applies (Pasal 5; PP 71 Pasal 29; Permen 20/2016 Pasal 28 huruf i).
- [ ] Categories of data subjects: makers/consumers, vendor natural persons, visitors.
- [ ] **Per purpose:** data types (general vs specific — Pasal 4), relevance, **basis** (Pasal 20 huruf a–f stated honestly), retention (Pasal 21 ayat (1) huruf d; PP 80 Pasal 38; then deletion Pasal 42–44).
- [ ] Processing activities in Pasal 16 ayat (1) language: collection, analysis, storage, disclosure, transfer, destruction.
- [ ] Recipients: internal roles, **named classes of vendors/processors/advertisers**, overseas locations (Pasal 55–56).
- [ ] **Vendor-marketing / sharing:** not described as part of “providing the service.” Separate purpose, separate consent, withdrawal (Pasal 22, 9, 40).
- [ ] Cross-border: actual mechanism used (adequacy / binding clauses / consent) (Pasal 56). Do not invent a ministerial whitelist.
- [ ] Rights list (Pasal 5–13) and **how to file a recorded request** (Pasal 14), including 3×24-hour clocks (Pasal 30, 32, 40, 41).
- [ ] Automated decision-making / profiling if used (Pasal 10, 34).
- [ ] Children: age limit, parental consent, ITE 16A mechanisms if the service is accessible to children (Pasal 25; ITE 16A).
- [ ] Security summary without overclaim (Pasal 35–39).
- [ ] Breach notification commitment consistent with Pasal 46 (3×24 hours), without promising a *lembaga* that cannot yet receive notices if it does not exist — state notice to the data subject in any event.
- [ ] How **this notice will be updated**: prior notice before changes (Pasal 21 ayat (2)); new consent for new optional purposes; **no** “continued use = consent”; version date and effective date.
- [ ] Complaint path: internal + Permendag 31/2023 Pasal 9–10 contacts; UU 27 Pasal 64 dispute routes (arbitration, court, other ADR).

### Consent UI (not the same as T&C tick)

- [ ] Bahasa Indonesia (Permen 20/2016 Pasal 7; *Penjelasan* UU 27 “bahasa”).
- [ ] Unbundled optional purposes (Pasal 22 ayat (4)).
- [ ] Not pre-ticked; not hidden (PP 71 *Penjelasan* Pasal 14 ayat (3)).
- [ ] Record who, when, what text was shown, what was accepted (Pasal 22, 24, 31).
- [ ] Withdrawal as easy to find as the grant (Pasal 9, 40).
- [ ] Third-party use **option** (Permen 20/2016 Pasal 28 huruf f).

### Terms & Conditions / User Agreement — Bahasa Indonesia; downloadable (PP 80 Pasal 55–56)

- [ ] Identity of the platform legal entity (PP 80 Pasal 13, 53; PP 71 Pasal 29).
- [ ] PP 80 Pasal 53 minimum commercial terms (object, price, pay, deliver, return, cancel, dispute law).
- [ ] Electronic acceptance step that is a **pernyataan penerimaan** (ITE Pasal 20), not browse-wrap.
- [ ] **No** Pasal 18 UU 8/1999 prohibited clauses (liability dump; unilateral mid-term rule changes; unreadably placed text).
- [ ] Change-of-terms process that does **not** force consumers to submit to new rules while using the service (Pasal 18 ayat (1) huruf g) — e.g. notice + ability to stop, not silent amendment.
- [ ] Link to Privacy Policy; **no** grant of marketing/share rights inside the T&C (UU 27 Pasal 23).
- [ ] Governing law: for consumer standard-form contracts with Indonesian users, ITE Pasal 18A points to **Indonesian law** when its connecting factors are met.
- [ ] Capacity / age gate aligned with Pasal 25 and ITE 16A.
- [ ] English version clearly marked as translation if offered (Perpres 63/2019 Pasal 26).

### Vendor contract / DPA

- [ ] Role: processor (Pasal 51) **or** joint controller (Pasal 18) **or** independent controller (Pasal 55) — pick one per data flow.
- [ ] Instructions, confidentiality, security, sub-processor written approval (Pasal 51 ayat (5)).
- [ ] Ban on using consumer data for the vendor’s own marketing unless the **consumer’s** separate consent exists and is passed through.
- [ ] Assistance with rights requests and breaches (Pasal 32, 46).
- [ ] Cross-border clauses if the vendor or its processors are abroad (Pasal 56).

### Governance (not text in the policy, but the statute requires the capability)

- [ ] Record of processing (Pasal 31).
- [ ] Proof of consents (Pasal 24).
- [ ] DPIA before high-risk features (Pasal 34) — especially vendor-marketing profiles, large-scale monitoring, specific data, new tracking tech.
- [ ] DPO assessment against Pasal 53.
- [ ] PSE registration (Permen 5/2020) and PMSE licensing (Permendag 31/2023) if not done.
- [ ] Retention schedule: purpose end vs PP 80 5/10-year holds vs Permen 20 5-year default — then delete (Pasal 42–45).
- [ ] Calendar: re-check JDIH/peraturan.go.id for a UU 27 implementing PP and for the Perpres establishing *lembaga* before locking “we will notify the data protection authority” wording.

### Honest sanctions language (optional in the policy; mandatory for internal risk)

Do not tell users that “Indonesian PDP is not yet in force.” Pasal 74’s adjustment ended 17 October 2024.

| Track | Maximum in the text | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Administrative (PDP) | Written warning; suspend processing; delete/destroy data; **fine up to 2% of annual revenue/receipts** against violation variables | UU 27 Pasal 57 |
| Criminal (unlawful obtain/collect causing loss) | 5 years and/or Rp 5 miliar | Pasal 65 ayat (1), 67 ayat (1) |
| Criminal (unlawful disclose) | 4 years and/or Rp 4 miliar | Pasal 65 ayat (2), 67 ayat (2) |
| Criminal (unlawful use) | 5 years and/or Rp 5 miliar | Pasal 65 ayat (3), 67 ayat (3) |

| PP 71 administrative | Listed in Pasal 100 of PP 71 (warning through administrative measures for, among others, Pasal 14 and Pasal 29 breaches) | PP 71 Pasal 100 |
| Permen 20/2016 administrative | Oral/written warning; temporary halt; publication on a website | Permen 20/2016 Pasal 34 |
